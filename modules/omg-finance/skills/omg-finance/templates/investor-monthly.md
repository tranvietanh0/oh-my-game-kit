# Investor Monthly Update — Template

Adapted Sequoia format. Fill variables in brackets. Target: 400–800 words. One page max.

---

**Subject:** [Company] — [Month Year] Investor Update

**From:** [Founder name]
**To:** [Investor distribution list]
**Date:** [Day of following month, typically 1st–7th]

---

## TL;DR

One or two sentences on the month. Lead with the most material thing — good or bad.

> Example: "March was our first cashflow-positive month; revenue +18% MoM to $62k and we crossed breakeven on the main business. Playable Labs concentration risk still elevated (Top-3 = 68%)."

## Highlights

3 bullets of wins. Specific, not generic.

- [Closed deal / shipped feature / key hire]
- [Traction or KPI milestone]
- [Strategic progress — partnership, market signal]

## Lowlights / risks

2–3 bullets, candid. Investors distrust all-green reports.

- [What didn't work and why]
- [Emerging risk — concentration, headcount, cash]
- [Regulatory / competitive / market]

## Unit economics (if material this period)

- [Payback period / LTV-CAC / ROAS]: [value] ([trend])

- Commentary: [1 sentence on health]

## Looking forward (next 30–60 days)

- [Top priority 1]

## Asks

Specific, actionable. Don't ask for "any intros" — ask for specific people.

- **Intros:** [name at company X — we're exploring Y]
- **Hires:** [role — we'd love to talk to specific profile]
- **Advice:** [specific question]

## Writer's checklist (before sending)

- [ ] Numbers reconciled against accounting system
- [ ] Variance vs plan explained for any >10% miss
- [ ] Currency labels present on every figure
- [ ] Runway uses net cash / trailing-3mo net burn
- [ ] One-time items separated from recurring in any trend claim
- [ ] Lowlights section has at least 2 items
- [ ] Asks are specific (names or specific profiles), not generic
- [ ] Email passes the "would I forward this" test for tone
- [ ] Sent before 7th of the month (ideally 1st–3rd)

## Writer's voice notes

- **Confidence without bravado.** State numbers; let them speak.
- **Candor on bad news.** Acknowledge, explain cause, say what you'll do.
- **No hedging language.** Don't write "we think" or "probably" — be clear or don't claim.
- **No jargon-for-jargon.** If you say "velocity" or "optionality," ask yourself if there's a plainer word.
- **Specific asks only.** Generic asks yield generic replies (none).
