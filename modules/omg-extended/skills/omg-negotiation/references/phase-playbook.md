# Phase Playbook — Multi-Round Negotiation Tactics

Phase-by-phase tactical guidance for multi-round negotiations. Each phase has: primary objective, must-do moves, anti-patterns to avoid, transition signal (when to advance to next phase).

---

## Phase 1 — Pre-negotiation (Days 1–7)

### Primary objective
Build BATNA, leverage map, cultural brief, and opening anchor BEFORE first contact. Most negotiations are won or lost before the first email.

### Must-do moves

**1. BATNA & reservation price analysis** (20 min)
- Write down your walk-away in plain numbers
- List counterparty's likely BATNA (3 sources: their public statements, similar recent deals, third-party intel)
- Calculate ZOPA (overlap zone)
- Identify 3–5 red lines (non-negotiable)
- Identify 3–5 green lights (what you want to win on)

**2. Leverage map** (15 min, Shell three-axis)
- Positive: top 3 things you can do FOR them
- Negative: top 2 things you can do TO them
- Normative: top 2 third-party expectations (with sources)
- Decide which lever to lead with

**3. Cultural briefing** (10 min)
- Look up counterparty's culture in `cultural-map.md`
- Identify trust style + disagreement style + pace
- Note 3 specific blunders to avoid

**4. Term sheet or opening position** (1–2 hours)
- Anchor first in writing (term letter, summary)
- Make anchor aggressive but defensible (cite precedent)
- Multi-dimensional anchor (3–5 dimensions simultaneously, never single-axis)

**5. Pre-mortem** (10 min)
- Imagine the deal failed 18 months after signing. Write 5 reasons why.
- Address top 2 reasons in your opening structure

### Anti-patterns

- **Skipping BATNA work** ("I'll figure it out as we go") — guarantees overcommitment under pressure
- **Single-axis anchor** (just royalty, just equity) — denies you trade dimensions in middle game
- **No cultural brief** — first interaction blunder costs 2-3 rounds to recover from
- **Open with apology or weakness** — "We know this is a big ask, but..." signals you'll cave

### Transition signal (advance to Phase 2)
You have a written opening position, BATNA documented, cultural brief, and a credible walk-away script. Counterparty has been notified you'll send opening. You are NOT ready if you're still trying to figure out your reservation price.

---

## Phase 2 — Opening (Days 7–14 / Round 1)

### Primary objective
Anchor in writing. Listen for real vs negotiating constraints. Excavate counterparty's underlying interests. Set the focal point for the entire negotiation.

### Must-do moves

**1. Send written opening clearly and unapologetically.**
- State your ask in 2-3 sentences max
- Explain rationale with normative + positive leverage (1-2 sentences each)
- Invite their counter in writing
- End with: "What's your read on structure? Any constraints I should know about?"

**2. Listen for constraints in their first reply.**
Real vs. negotiating constraints are easy to distinguish:

| Real constraint | Negotiating constraint |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| "Our legal doc requires board approval of equity > 25%" | "We can't do less than 40% — last deal was 50%" |
| "Korean tax law treats foreign equity differently" | "Industry standard is 30%" |
| "Our investor mandate requires X" | "We need this number to look good" |

When you hear real constraints, acknowledge and plan around. When you hear negotiating constraints, push back politely.

**3. Interest excavation (Voss / Fisher-Ury combined).**
For every position they state, ask "Why?". Probe for underlying interest.

*Example:*
- Position: "We want 40% equity for Phase 1 funding"
- Real interest (revealed by probing): "We want de facto control over milestone acceptance"
- Counter: solve the interest with control mechanism (e.g., milestone veto), not by giving 40% equity

**4. Mirror and label.**
When they anchor, mirror their last 3 words: "40% equity?" (let them elaborate or entrench).
When they state a concern, label: "You're worried about long-term commitment from us."

### Anti-patterns

- **Negotiating with whoever picks up the phone** — verify decision authority. If they're not the decider, request the actual authority be in the room.
- **Same-day responses** under pressure — gives no time to think; pre-commit to 24-hour response rule for substantive emails
- **Conceding before they ask** — your opening must hold; don't soften it preemptively
- **Filling silence** — after your anchor, shut up. Let them respond first.

### Transition signal (advance to Phase 3)
Both parties have anchored. You've identified 2-3 of their interests (not just positions). You have a feel for their negotiation style and posture. You're ready for trades.

---

## Phase 3 — Middle game (Rounds 2–7)

### Primary objective
Test concession bands. Bundle multi-dimensional trades. Apply Karrass decreasing-step discipline. Send written recaps after every round.

This is where most negotiations are won or lost. Opening is theater; closing is celebration. Middle game is the real fight.

### Must-do moves

**1. Concession testing & incremental trades.**
- Round 2: Concede on lowest-value ask to signal movement. Ask for something new on high-value.
- Round 3-4: Test concession bands (do they budge on equity? milestones? territory?)
- Rounds 5-6: Bundle. Every concession trades.

**2. Decreasing-step concession discipline.**
- Round 2 concession: largest, most visible move
- Round 3 concession: ~50% of round 2
- Round 4-5 concessions: small tweaks only

If you concede in increasing or constant size, you signal infinite headroom. Document your concession sequence in writing BEFORE each round.

**3. Voss tactical empathy moves.**
- Mirror when they anchor: lets them elaborate or entrench
- Label when they show emotion: defuses by naming
- Calibrated questions: "What would it take to get to..." not "Will you accept..."
- "That's right" check after reframings

**4. Written recaps after each round.**
After every meaningful exchange, send a one-page recap:
- What's agreed
- What's open
- Next round topics
- Definitions clarified

This hardens agreements, surfaces misunderstandings early, and creates the paper trail you'll need if disputes emerge later.

**5. Multi-dimensional bundling.**
Never trade single issues 1:1. Always bundle 3-5 dimensions:
- Royalty + milestones + approval rights + timeline + territory
- Equity + vesting cliff + pool size + Pool A KPIs + Pool B exit triggers

Pre-build 3-4 bundle scenarios. Each bundle = different path to mutual gain.

### Anti-patterns

- **Capitulation under pressure** — when they push hard, take 24 hours before responding. Most pressure is theater.
- **Forgetting your concession sequence** — if you don't write it down, you'll concede more than planned
- **One-way concessions** — "I move on X, what do you move on?" should be your reflex
- **Neglecting the recap** — verbal agreements rot fast; written recaps are the antidote
- **Reactive devaluation** — assuming their proposal must be bad because they made it. Evaluate against your BATNA, not their motivation.

### Transition signal (advance to Phase 4)
You've identified 3-5 remaining open issues. Concession testing has revealed their priorities. You can predict 2 of their next 3 moves. You're ready to bundle and close.

---

## Phase 4 — Closing (Rounds 7–10)

### Primary objective
Bundle final trades. Defend against late-stage nibbling. Maintain walk-away credibility. Push for signed term sheet or LOI.

### Must-do moves

**1. Bundle the final trades.**
- List remaining open issues (usually 3-5)
- Propose multi-dimensional bundle: "If we do 12% royalty + quarterly bonus + 5-year non-compete waiver, can we close?"
- Let them choose order of importance among bundle elements

**2. Final round announcement.**
- "Round 8 is our final round on these terms."
- "Any new asks will need to be bundled with concessions we ask for."
- Then enforce it.

**3. Nibble defense.**
In rounds 8-9, expect counterparty to introduce small new asks. Each individually seems minor; cumulatively they reset the ZOPA. Refuse all nibbles or force trades.

**4. Walk-away credibility.**
If ZOPA has collapsed (their range no longer overlaps yours), walk. State professionally: "I think we need different terms. Let's revisit in 2 weeks." (Not: "This deal is dead.")

Often this catalyzes a counteroffer.

**5. Push to signed term sheet.**
Before moving to definitive agreement, get a signed term sheet or LOI. This locks in the substantive terms and protects against retroactive redefinition in the legal phase.

**6. Definition lock.**
Before closing, freeze every definition that matters: "Net Revenue", "Qualifying Client", "Material Breach", "Change of Control", etc. Attach worked examples (3 positive + 3 negative).

### Anti-patterns

- **Bluffing the walk-away** — if you threaten to walk and don't, you've burned the move forever
- **Accepting nibbles to "just close"** — every late nibble was high-value enough to wait for; refuse or trade
- **Skipping the term sheet** — going straight to definitive agreement loses leverage; legal phase will renegotiate everything
- **Verbal agreements** — "we agreed in the call" is not enforceable; everything in writing
- **Closing on Friday afternoon** — decision fatigue. Push hard close to morning sessions.

### Transition signal (advance to Phase 5)
Term sheet signed (or LOI executed). All material terms locked. Definitions frozen. Both teams aligned on next steps for legal drafting.

---

## Phase 5 — Post-signature (Weeks 1–12)

### Primary objective
Build relationship. Prevent disputes. Lock definitions before legal renders them. Prepare for potential round 2 (renegotiation in N years).

### Must-do moves

**1. Relationship deepening.**
- First 30 days: weekly or bi-weekly check-in (informal OK)
- Build personal relationship with their team
- Public acknowledgment of their wins ("XYZ Publisher made a smart move on localization")
- Respond to all messages within 24 hours

**2. Pre-legal alignment.**
- Send the legal team your interpretation of every ambiguous term sheet clause
- Resolve interpretation differences BEFORE the legal team starts drafting
- Lock all definitions in writing — no further discussion in legal phase

**3. Dispute prevention.**
- Proactively flag any interpretation differences when you spot them
- Document any verbal agreements in writing immediately
- If they push back on legal language, circle back to term-sheet INTENT rather than fighting over prose

**4. Cultural relationship-deepening (per Meyer).**
- For relationship-cultures (Korea, Vietnam, Turkey, China): invest in social bonding
- For task-cultures (Germany, US): demonstrate competence + delivery
- Both: build credibility for round 2 of relationship (which always comes)

**5. KPI monitoring.**
If the deal involves milestones, KPIs, or earn-outs, set up monitoring infrastructure NOW. The fight in 12 months will be about whether KPI X was hit. Without monitoring data, you lose the fight regardless of substantive merit.

### Anti-patterns

- **Going silent after signing** — feels like loss of attention; counterparty defaults assume worst
- **Letting legal drift away from term sheet intent** — every drift point becomes a future dispute
- **Accepting "we'll figure it out later"** on definitions — you won't; ambiguities will be weaponized
- **Skipping KPI monitoring setup** — "we'll start tracking when we need to" = you'll never have the data when needed

### Transition signal (move to operational mode)
Legal agreement signed. KPI monitoring infrastructure live. Both teams operationalizing the deal. Round 1 of negotiation is complete; round 2 (renegotiation, expansion, exit) starts the clock.

## Phase-specific Voss scripts

Tactical empathy phrases tailored to each phase:

### Phase 1 (Pre-negotiation, internal)
- (Internal): "If they push back on equity, what's our fallback?"
- (Internal): "What's our walk-away script — exact words?"

### Phase 2 (Opening)
- "What would a successful partnership look like from your side?"
- "What's been your experience with [similar deals]?"
- (After their anchor) "{Last 3 of their words}?"

### Phase 3 (Middle game)
- "How do we structure this so [their concern] is addressed and [our concern] is also addressed?"
- "It sounds like the real issue is [interest], not [position]?"
- (After your concession) "What are you moving on in return?"
- "Is that right?" (after a reframing)

### Phase 4 (Closing)
- "We're at the line. What do we need to bundle to close this?"
- "I know it's a big package. What single change would make this a yes?"
- (Walk-away script) "I don't think we're going to get there on these terms. Let's pause for 2 weeks and see if either side has new thinking."

### Phase 5 (Post-signature)
- "How's it landing on your team?"
- "What's the most important thing for the first 90 days?"
- "What would success look like at the 1-year mark?"

## Round-counter mental model

Most multi-round negotiations follow a predictable count:

| Round | Phase | Energy/posture |
|-------|-------|----------------|
| 0 (pre) | Phase 1 | Preparation |
| 1 | Phase 2 (opening) | Anchor + listen |
| 2 | Phase 3 (early middle) | First concession + first ask |
| 3-4 | Phase 3 | Test concession bands |
| 5-6 | Phase 3 (late middle) | Bundle proposals |
| 7-8 | Phase 4 (closing) | Final bundle + nibble defense |
| 9 | Phase 4 | Term sheet signature |
| 10+ | Phase 5 | Legal + relationship |

If a negotiation is at round 5 and feels like Phase 4 closing energy, you're rushing. If at round 5 and feels like Phase 2 opening energy, you're stalling. Diagnose where you actually are vs. where the round count suggests.

## Phase-specific BATNA recalculation

Don't just calculate BATNA at Phase 1. Recalculate at:

- **Phase 2 → Phase 3 transition:** Has counterparty's behavior changed your assessment of their BATNA? Has your BATNA changed (new alternative emerged)?
- **Phase 3 mid-game (Round 4-5):** The danger zone. Sunk cost is high; emotional investment is high. Recalculate as if Day 1.
- **Phase 4 (final round):** Last check before signing. Does the deal still clear your reservation price?
- **Post-signature (Phase 5):** What's your BATNA for renegotiation in 12 months? Start building it now.

The single highest-leverage move in any multi-round negotiation: recalculate BATNA at round 4-5. Most bad deals get signed because nobody recalculated when they should have.
