# Mode 5 — `schedule-a` (non-compete genre scope)

When §Non-compete references a Schedule A but none is attached, draft one with four sections.

The full template lives at `references/schedule-a-template.md`. This file documents the drafting workflow and rules.

## Four required sections

1. **A.1 In-scope categories** — game categories and genres actively operated by Company.
2. **A.2 Out-of-scope carve-outs** — what Partner may freely serve.
3. **A.3 Annual update mechanism** — how A.1/A.2 lists evolve over time.
4. **A.4 Interpretation rules** — three-conjunctive test (genre AND audience AND platform) + edge-case resolution.

## Drafting rules

1. **Be specific in A.1** — "mobile games" is too broad and unenforceable. Name genres: hyper-casual, casual puzzle, match-3, hybrid-casual, mini-game collections, playable-ad content, etc.
2. **Be generous in A.2** — the more you explicitly carve out, the more enforceable the in-scope list becomes. Err toward over-carving.
3. **Three-conjunctive test in A.4** — genre AND audience AND platform. This prevents "you have a puzzle mini-game, therefore no mobile puzzle" overreach.
4. **Annual update, not one-shot** — the categories evolve; the mechanism must too. Without A.3, the schedule ossifies and becomes a liability when Company changes direction.
5. **Edge-case resolution in favor of Partner** — A.4 should default ambiguous cases to Partner unless Company demonstrates material commercial overlap with a then-active Company title. This calibrates the enforceability range.

## Common drafting mistakes to avoid

- **A.1 listing platform-only constraints** (e.g., "all mobile games") — unenforceable per OECD non-compete jurisprudence; must constrain by genre.
- **A.2 missing entirely** — without explicit carve-outs, A.1 reads as exhaustive and becomes vulnerable to "implied unbounded scope" attack at enforcement.
- **A.3 with no specific dates or notice mechanism** — annual update needs concrete trigger (anniversary date), notice period (30 days), and a dispute-escalation pointer.
- **A.4 silent on multi-genre titles** — many real games span 2-3 genres. A.4 must say which test wins (primary genre? mechanic? marketing?).

## When to draft Schedule A

- Non-compete clause references "Schedule A" but no attachment exists in the draft.
- Counterparty asks for genre scope to be specified.
- You're the drafting party and want to constrain the non-compete range proactively (better Partner-side optics + better enforceability).

Do NOT draft Schedule A if:
- The non-compete is firm-level only (no individual scope) — Schedule A isn't needed.
- Counterparty has already drafted a Schedule A; review theirs first via `tilt` mode.
- The contract is in a non-compete-void jurisdiction (e.g., California, Vietnam labor law) — focus on firm-level fallback instead.
