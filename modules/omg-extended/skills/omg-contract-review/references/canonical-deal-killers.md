# Canonical deal-killer clauses

Across many contract reviews, these clauses recur as deal-killers when present in the adversarial form. Always scan for these in `tilt` mode and always fix in `balance` R1.

1. **Voluntary exit forfeits vested equity** — always remove. Vested equity is sacrosanct; only breach/fraud can claw it back.
2. **Non-compete >18 months with worldwide scope and no genre carve-out** — unenforceable in OECD jurisdictions; always narrow scope or reduce duration.
3. **Clawback with no fair-cause exceptions** — must carve out Company-caused churn (product failure, unilateral pricing, direction pivot, M&A-caused merge).
4. **Unilateral termination with <30-day notice and no acceleration** — must add notice period + acceleration formula (greater-of 50% unvested OR 100% next tranche is a defensible middle).
5. **Gross margin / cost-of-sales as a vesting gate for a sales partner** — Partner cannot control Company's delivery cost; demote to reported metric.
6. **Pool B (exit) with a minimum-valuation cliff** — always replace with a graduated ladder starting at a nominal % below the threshold.
7. **IP assignment with no carve-out for Partner's pre-existing methodologies** — always carve out frameworks Partner brought in.
8. **"Material breach" undefined in for-cause termination** — always define as "(a) breach that materially impairs the counterparty's economic position AND (b) not cured within 30 days of written notice with reasonable specificity." Undefined "material" is an arbitration-bait clause.
9. **Exit / valuation ladder without strict `≥` and `<` boundary inequalities** — always rewrite ranges like "$4M–$6M" as `≥ $4M and < $6M`, with an explicit note about which tier boundary values belong to. Ambiguous boundaries are arbitration bait at the moment of exit.
10. **Anti-non-compete jurisdictions without firm-level fallback** — Vietnam, California, some EU states void individual non-competes. Always add: "Where a Key Person's domicile law renders the individual non-compete unenforceable, (a) the firm-level non-compete remains enforceable against Partner firm, (b) the firm-level clawback remedy applies, (c) Partner firm is jointly and severally liable for resulting damage."
