# Schedule A — Non-Compete Genre Scope Template

A 4-section template for drafting a non-compete genre-scope appendix. Use when the non-compete clause references a Schedule A but no schedule is attached.

This template enforces the **3-conjunctive test** (genre AND audience AND platform) which is the single most important enforceability lever in non-compete drafting.

---

## Template

```markdown
## Schedule A — Non-Compete Genre Scope (per §{N})

> *Illustrative draft. To be finalized by mutual written agreement at execution
> and updated every 12 months thereafter.*

### A.1 Game categories and genres actively operated by Company (in-scope for §{N} non-compete)

As of the Effective Date, Company's active game categories and genres include:

1. **{Genre category 1}** — {specific mechanics/subgenres/audience target}
2. **{Genre category 2}** — {specific mechanics/subgenres/audience target}
3. **{Genre category 3}** — {specific mechanics/subgenres/audience target}
4. **{Genre category 4}** — {specific mechanics/subgenres/audience target}
5. **{Genre category 5}** — {specific mechanics/subgenres/audience target}

### A.2 Explicitly out-of-scope (Partner may freely serve)

Partner is unrestricted with respect to:

- **{Category 1}** — {specific subgenres + reason out-of-scope}
- **{Category 2}** — {specific subgenres + reason out-of-scope}
- **{Category 3}** — {specific subgenres + reason out-of-scope}
- **{Category 4}** — {specific subgenres + reason out-of-scope}
- **{Category 5}** — {specific subgenres + reason out-of-scope}
- **{Category 6}** — {specific subgenres + reason out-of-scope}
- **{Category 7}** — {specific subgenres + reason out-of-scope}
- **{Category 8}** — {specific subgenres + reason out-of-scope}

### A.3 Annual update mechanism

Per §{N}, this Schedule A is reviewed and updated annually by mutual written agreement on each anniversary of the Effective Date.

- **Additions:** Company may propose adding a genre to §A.1 when Company commercially enters that genre (live or in soft launch), with 30 days' written notice to Partner.
- **Removals:** Partner may propose moving a genre from §A.1 to §A.2 if Company has materially exited the genre (no active titles in the category for ≥ 12 months).
- **Disputes:** Escalate per §{dispute section} (HKIAC arbitration, Hong Kong seat).

### A.4 Interpretation rules

- **"Directly competing"** means a game in the same primary genre AND targeting the same primary audience segment AND shipping on the same primary platform. **All three conditions must hold.**
- A game that shares a mechanic with Company's portfolio but sits in a different primary genre (e.g., a puzzle mini-game embedded in a midcore RPG) is **not** directly competing.
- A game in a Company genre but on a distinct platform (e.g., a console-only match-3 title) is out of scope.
- A game on the same platform, same genre, but targeting a fundamentally different audience segment (e.g., children's edutainment puzzle game vs. Company's adult-targeted match-3) is out of scope.
- Ambiguous edge cases are resolved in favor of Partner unless Company demonstrates material commercial overlap with a then-active Company title.
```

## Drafting rules

### 1. Be specific in A.1
"Mobile games" is too broad and unenforceable. Name genres specifically:
- ❌ "Mobile games"
- ❌ "Casual games"
- ✅ "Hyper-casual mobile games — simple-mechanic, short-session games designed for broad mobile audiences (e.g., tap-to-play, runners, idle, reaction-based, ASMR)"
- ✅ "Casual puzzle mobile games — match-3, line-connect, swap-tile, block-fitting, and similar low-complexity puzzle formats"
- ✅ "Hybrid-casual mobile games — hyper-casual core loops combined with light meta-progression (upgrades, collections, soft economy)"
- ✅ "Mini-game collections — aggregated casual game experiences delivered as compilation apps"
- ✅ "Playable-ad content — interactive advertising units derived from or adjacent to Company's game properties"

### 2. Be generous in A.2
The more you explicitly carve out, the more enforceable the in-scope list becomes. Err toward over-carving:

- **Midcore and hardcore mobile games** — RPGs, strategy (4X, RTS), MMO, shooters, fighting, sports simulations, racing simulations
- **PC-exclusive games** — desktop/laptop titles not shipped on mobile
- **Console-exclusive games** — PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo Switch, or other dedicated console titles not shipped on mobile
- **B2B SaaS and enterprise software** — tools, platforms, and services outside consumer gaming
- **Web3 / crypto / NFT games** — blockchain-integrated game properties
- **Card games, TCG, deck-builders** — trading card games, collectible card games, and digital card-based strategy titles
- **Simulation and tycoon games** — business sims, life sims, city builders, farming sims (unless and until Company enters this category)
- **Educational / edutainment apps** — learning-first content
- **Non-game entertainment apps** — video, music, social, creator tools

### 3. Three-conjunctive test in A.4
Genre AND audience AND platform. This prevents "you have a puzzle mini-game, therefore no mobile puzzle" overreach. All three conditions must hold for "directly competing".

Examples:
- Same genre + same audience + same platform = directly competing ✓
- Same genre + different audience = NOT directly competing
- Same genre + same audience + different platform = NOT directly competing
- Different genre + same audience + same platform = NOT directly competing

### 4. Annual update, not one-shot
The categories evolve; the mechanism must too. Without A.3 (annual update), the schedule ossifies and becomes a liability when Company changes direction.

Pattern: Schedule A is a living document. Both parties have proposal rights with notice period and dispute resolution.

## Filled example — gaming partnership (for The One Game Studio)

```markdown
## Schedule A — Non-Compete Genre Scope (per §9)

### A.1 Game categories and genres actively operated by Company (in-scope for §9 non-compete)

As of the Effective Date, Company's active game categories and genres include:

1. **Hyper-casual mobile games** — simple-mechanic, short-session games designed
   for broad mobile audiences (e.g., tap-to-play, runners, idle, reaction-based, ASMR)
2. **Casual puzzle mobile games** — match-3, line-connect, swap-tile,
   block-fitting, and similar low-complexity puzzle formats
3. **Hybrid-casual mobile games** — hyper-casual core loops combined with
   light meta-progression (upgrades, collections, soft economy)
4. **Mini-game collections** — aggregated casual game experiences delivered
   as compilation apps
5. **Playable-ad content** — interactive advertising units derived from
   or adjacent to Company's game properties

### A.2 Explicitly out-of-scope (Partner may freely serve)

Partner is unrestricted with respect to:

- **Midcore and hardcore mobile games** — RPGs, strategy (4X, RTS), MMO,
  shooters, fighting, sports simulations, racing simulations
- **PC-exclusive games** — desktop/laptop titles not shipped on mobile
- **Console-exclusive games** — PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo Switch, or other
  dedicated console titles not shipped on mobile
- **B2B SaaS and enterprise software** — tools, platforms, and services
  outside consumer gaming
- **Web3 / crypto / NFT games** — blockchain-integrated game properties
- **Card games, TCG, deck-builders** — trading card games, collectible card
  games, and digital card-based strategy titles
- **Simulation and tycoon games** — business sims, life sims, city builders,
  farming sims (unless and until Company enters this category)
- **Educational / edutainment apps** — learning-first content
- **Non-game entertainment apps** — video, music, social, creator tools

### A.3 Annual update mechanism

Per §9, this Schedule A is reviewed and updated annually by mutual written
agreement on each anniversary of the Effective Date.

- **Additions:** Company may propose adding a genre to §A.1 when Company
  commercially enters that genre (live or in soft launch), with 30 days'
  written notice to Partner.
- **Removals:** Partner may propose moving a genre from §A.1 to §A.2 if
  Company has materially exited the genre (no active titles in the category
  for ≥ 12 months).
- **Disputes:** Escalate per §17 (HKIAC arbitration, Hong Kong seat).

### A.4 Interpretation rules

- **"Directly competing"** means a game in the same primary genre AND
  targeting the same primary audience segment AND shipping on mobile.
  All three conditions must hold.
- A game that shares a mechanic with Company's portfolio but sits in a
  different primary genre (e.g., a puzzle mini-game embedded in a midcore
  RPG) is **not** directly competing.
- A game in a Company genre but on a distinct platform (e.g., a console-only
  match-3 title) is out of scope.
- Ambiguous edge cases are resolved in favor of Partner unless Company
  demonstrates material commercial overlap with a then-active Company title.
```

## When NOT to use this template

- **Standard NDAs** — Schedule A is for non-compete clauses, not confidentiality
- **Distribution agreements with no non-compete** — if no non-compete, no need for Schedule A
- **Highly regulated jurisdictions** (Vietnam labor law, California) — non-competes voided regardless; Schedule A doesn't help. Need different protection mechanism (firm-level non-compete + joint-and-several liability — see `deal-killer-checklist.md` item #10)
